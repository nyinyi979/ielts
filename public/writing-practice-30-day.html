01

How to use the 30-day pack

Practise under exam timing first. Open the model only after you have planned or written your own response.

Daily timing

  • Task 1: 20 minutes, 150+ words.
  • Task 2: 40 minutes, 250+ words.
  • Keep 3–5 minutes for checking.
  • On later days, complete both tasks without pausing.

Compare decisions, not fancy words

  • Find the Task 1 overview before reading mine.
  • Write two Task 2 main-idea sentences first.
  • Compare paragraph jobs and development.
  • Reuse useful collocations only when they fit naturally.
Important
These are study models, not official IELTS-rated scripts, so no exact band score can be guaranteed. The goal is to demonstrate the clarity, development, selection and language control associated with stronger performance.
02

What the models target

The same four assessment areas used in IELTS Writing: task achievement/response, coherence and cohesion, lexical resource, and grammatical range and accuracy.

Task 1

  1. Paraphrase what the visual shows.
  2. Give a genuine overview of the most important features.
  3. Group related details rather than describing everything.
  4. Use figures selectively to support comparisons.

Task 2

  1. Answer the exact question in the introduction.
  2. Give each paragraph a clear purpose.
  3. Explain ideas before adding examples.
  4. Keep the position consistent through the conclusion.
03

Your 30 practice days

Each day contains one real Task 1 visual and one Task 2 essay question. The model answers stay collapsed until you choose to open them.

04

How to study the model answers

The highest-value exercise is reconstructing the thinking, not memorising sentences.

  1. Write or plan the question before opening the accordion.
  2. Underline the model's thesis or overview.
  3. Write 3–5 words beside each paragraph describing its job.
  4. Circle useful collocations, not isolated “advanced” words.
  5. Close the answer and rewrite the same argument in your own language.
  6. One week later, answer the question again under full timing.
Do not memorise whole essays. Learn the decisions behind them: what to select, what to omit, how to group information, and how to develop a position naturally.
