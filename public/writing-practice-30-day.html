How to use this page

Practise first

  • Task 1: 20 minutes · 150+ words
  • Task 2: 40 minutes · 250+ words
  • Plan before you open the model answer.
  • Compare decisions and paragraph jobs, not individual fancy words.

What the models target

  • A clear Task 1 overview and selective comparisons
  • A clear, developed Task 2 position
  • Natural progression without excessive linking phrases
  • Precise vocabulary rather than forced “advanced” words
  • Varied grammar that stays controlled

Important: These are study models written to demonstrate features associated with strong IELTS Writing performance; they are not official IELTS answers and no exact band score can be guaranteed. The Task 1 visuals are reusable charts and diagrams from Wikimedia Commons and Our World in Data, with source links under each image.

Task 1 structure

  1. Paraphrase what the visual shows.
  2. Give a real overview: the 2–3 features that matter most.
  3. Group related details and compare them.
  4. Use numbers selectively; do not narrate every point.

Task 2 structure

  1. Answer the exact question in the introduction.
  2. Give each body paragraph a clear purpose.
  3. Explain why the idea matters before adding an example.
  4. End with a conclusion that matches the argument you actually made.

Why these models are written this way

IELTS assesses Task Achievement/Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Stronger bands require relevant coverage, clear development, effective organisation, precise and flexible language, and increasingly strong grammatical control. Task 2 also carries twice the weight of Task 1 in the Writing score.

How to study the model answers

  1. Write or plan the question before opening the accordion.
  2. Underline the model's thesis or overview.
  3. Write 3–5 words beside each paragraph describing its job.
  4. Circle useful collocations, not impressive-looking isolated words.
  5. Close the answer and rewrite the same argument in your own language.
  6. One week later, answer the question again under full timing.

Do not memorise whole essays. Reproducing memorised language can make your response less flexible and may fail to answer the exact task. Learn the thinking pattern instead.
